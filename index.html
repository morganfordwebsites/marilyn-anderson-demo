No printing at the last minute, no second trip because a date was missed, no queue at a bank branch. Three steps and it is done.

I witness the signatures, complete the notarial certificate, seal it and log it in the journal. You keep a copy of everything, and I leave with nothing but the journal entry.

What to have on the table

  • Valid, unexpired government photo ID for each signer
  • The full document, complete but not yet signed
  • Every signer present — notarization cannot be done for someone absent
  • Witnesses, if the document calls for them
  • The signing instructions, for loan and closing packages
  • Payment for the appointment

Bringing a document that is already signed and notarized incorrectly usually means starting over. Text me a photo of the signature page beforehand if you are unsure.

What do I need to bring?

A valid, unexpired government photo ID — driver's license, passport or state ID — for every person signing. The document itself, complete but unsigned, plus any witnesses the document requires. If the document is a loan or closing package, bring the signing instructions too.

Can you tell me which notarial act my document needs?

I can tell you what the certificate on the page calls for and whether it is filled out correctly. I cannot tell you which document to use or how to word it — that is legal advice, and as a notary I am not permitted to give it. When in doubt, the attorney who prepared the document decides.

Do you come to hospitals and care facilities?

Yes, and often at short notice. Bedside signings for hospital patients, hospice residents and assisted living are a regular part of the work. Tell me the facility, the room if you have it, and who to coordinate with at the desk. I keep those appointments quiet and unhurried.

Someone cannot sign in person. What are the options?

Two options. Remote online notarization, where we meet by video and I seal electronically — the signer needs a camera and photo ID. Or, if the signer is physically able to make a mark but cannot travel, I come to them. If the person cannot sign or direct someone to sign for them at all, that is a legal question for an attorney, not a notary.

What is RON, exactly?

Remote online notarization: a video appointment where identity is verified electronically, the signature is sealed with an electronic seal and the whole session is recorded, as Tennessee requires. It is useful when a signer is out of state or housebound. I am set up for it and it is $30 for a standard session.

Can you notarize a will in Tennessee?

Wills are witnessed, not notarized, in Tennessee — but the self-proving affidavit that goes with a will is notarized, and that is what saves your family a trip to probate court later. I handle the affidavit, coordinate the two witnesses and make sure the signing order is right.

How far do you travel, and what does it cost?

Gallatin, Hendersonville and the rest of Sumner County are included in the appointment price. Nashville, Mt. Juliet and Lebanon add $25. Franklin, Murfreesboro and Clarksville add $45. Beyond 45 miles I quote the trip before you commit. Hospital and facility visits follow the same zones.

How do I pay, and can I reschedule?

Payment is collected at the appointment; I will confirm the accepted methods when we book. Rescheduling is free up to 12 hours before the appointment. Inside 12 hours, a same-day cancellation usually still owes the trip fee, since the slot was held.

Do you perform weddings as well?

Yes. I am a wedding officiant as well as a notary, so I can perform the ceremony, sign the license and return it to the county clerk — one less thing for the couple to chase. Elopements start at $250 and I travel across Middle Tennessee.
